Scoring Rules & Adjustments

Score = Σ(wᵢ × Partᵢ) × (VDP ? 0.75 : 1.0)
Within platform ranked Unknown part = 45 VDP ×0.75 Paused/closed gated Own reports excluded

{{T "The parts are averaged by their weights, and a program that pays no bounties has that average multiplied by 0.75: the four parts of a disclosure-only program weigh to a higher number than the score it shows, and its program page says so under the parts."}}

Fresh

35% default

In-scope assets added in the last 30 days, weighted by kind (wildcards highest), boosted for a root domain new to the program, fading by half every 14 days; plus recent invites, launches, reopenings, or raised bounty tables.

Competition

30% default

Report volume for scope size, ranked within the platform (fewer reports scores higher), with a boost for private programs. Uses H1 90d reports, YWH/Intigriti derived 90d, BC CrowdStream 90d (or rewarded delta scaled if private).

Payout

25% default

Top bounty, bounties paid in 90 days, and average bounty ranked within the platform. Non-paying (VDP) programs score 0 here and have their overall HuntScore multiplied by 0.75.

Response

10% default

Share of reports answered on time, time to first response, and time to validate, ranked within the platform. Missing metrics default to 45 (unknown) and reduce score confidence.

Parts are ranked within one platform so differing metrics never mix across platforms. Paused and closed programs keep their score but are left out of hunt board picks. A newly accessed private program dates its whole scope when you joined (or when HuntDeck first listed it), so older private scopes still surface as fresh targets.

Published, counted and estimated

Source definitions, short-format rounding, and daily snapshot cadence (~20h)

Published numbers are the platform's own figures, as it shows them. Counted numbers HuntDeck works out itself from report dates the platform lists. Estimates are marked ≈ and shown fainter; hover one for its working. Where a platform has nothing that measures the same thing, the cell shows a dash (—) rather than a different measure in its place; hover it to see that the platform doesn't share the number. A dash means only that: a zero shows as 0, and a program that pays no bounties says so on its money cells.

Amounts are shown short ("$450", "$8.3k", "$1.4M") and durations in at most two units ("7d 18h", "51d", "8mo 1w"); hover an amount for it to the dollar. An estimate stays short even there, as it isn't that exact.

Platform stats are saved once a day, at the first full poll after about 20 hours. Intigriti's come from its public program pages, since its researcher API has none, or, with the optional web login (IT_EMAIL), from the logged-in app, which also covers private and invite-only programs and adds hunters, response times, the bounty table and the program's updates. Most of them are totals the platform keeps current, so their history starts with HuntDeck's first save and fills in a day at a time; it can't be filled in for earlier days.

Total paid

H1 & Intigriti publish totals; Bugcrowd estimates from rewarded × recent average

HackerOne publishes each program's bounties paid since it started. The day-to-day rise is what the program paid that day.

Intigriti publishes the total paid on each public program's page; programs you have to apply to, or are invited to, have no public page, so they show a dash unless the web login is set up.

Bugcrowd publishes no total, only how many vulnerabilities a program has rewarded (all time) and its average payout (over the last three months). Their product estimates the total. It tends to run high, since it applies a recent average to every reward ever given, and the count may include rewards that weren't cash. It also moves as the three-month average does, even on days without payouts.

Average bounty

H1 range (midpoint sort), BC 3-month avg, YWH EUR subset, Intigriti public

Each platform's own figure, and they differ: HackerOne gives a range ("$200 – $300"), Bugcrowd its average payout over the last three months, YesWeHack an average reward, for a few programs only, in euros, and Intigriti its average payout, on public programs' pages. The list shows them short ("$1.6k – $2.5k"); hover for the exact amounts. Sorting by it ranks HackerOne's ranges by their middle.

Paid · 90 days

H1 published; Intigriti delta/leaderboard backfill; BC CrowdStream or rewarded delta

HackerOne publishes bounties paid in the last 90 days. The chart is that figure, one point per saved day.

Intigriti publishes its total paid, so paid in 90 days is worked out from it like reports received: counted once HuntDeck has saved 90 days of totals, estimated (≈) before that in the same ways. On the Stats page its line goes back a year before the first saved day, a point at each month's start (≈): the three months before's accepted reports, estimated from the program's monthly leaderboards (see valid reports per month), times what the program pays per accepted report (its total paid over its accepted reports, all time). It joins the saved days where a program's figure is sound: counted, or from its leaderboard share. Programs that don't publish their total paid, or don't have a sound figure yet, are left out of it.

The Stats page's Paid line is the programs paying in the currency most is paid in, which depends on the programs shown: for Intigriti that is euros under Private, where the line runs back a year from the estimates above, and dollars under All and Public, where most of the public programs paying in dollars have no sound figure yet, so the line starts at the first saved day and reaches further back as programs get one. The amounts in other currencies show beside the figure, as they can't be added together.

Bugcrowd publishes no such figure, but CrowdStream lists each report a program accepted in the last six months, and the program's average payout covers the last three. The estimate is the accepted reports in the last 90 days, leaving out priority 5 (informational, seldom paid), times that average, so both cover the same window. It is at least what CrowdStream itself shows, since CrowdStream shows the bounty on only some reports. A program's chart on the Programs list uses each day's saved average (days before HuntDeck's first save use the earliest one); the Stats page's line, which adds up every program, works each past day out at today's average. Limits: public programs only; a report can be accepted one month and paid the next; and some accepted reports earn points rather than money.

Some Bugcrowd programs keep CrowdStream to themselves: it says "no accepted or disclosed submissions" while the program publishes hundreds of rewarded vulnerabilities. HuntDeck treats a feed that has never listed a report that way, not as a program without reports, so its accepted and resolved counts show a dash. Paid in 90 days is then the rise in the program's count of rewarded vulnerabilities, saved daily, times its average payout (≈ always): scaled up from a week or more of counts until there are 90 days of them, and the whole count for a program launched less than 90 days ago.

Reports received · 90 days

Competition gauge: H1 published; YWH & Intigriti diffed daily (with 7d/leaderboard ramp)

HackerOne publishes every report received in the last 90 days, duplicates and invalid ones included, which makes it a measure of competition: more is more hunters on the program. Bugcrowd publishes nothing like it; its accepted (valid) reports are a much smaller number, so they stay on the program's own page.

YesWeHack and Intigriti publish every report a program has ever received, duplicates and invalid ones included. HuntDeck saves that total daily, and with 90 days of it the figure is counted: today's total less the one from 90 days before. Until then it is an estimate (≈):

  • YesWeHack: the reports over the days saved so far, plus its own last-7-days count for the rest of the 90 days (the first day, that count × 90/7).
  • Intigriti, with the web login: the all-time total × the share of the program's leaderboard points earned in the last 90 days. Each accepted report earns fixed points by severity (low 5 to exceptional 50; a duplicate a fifth of that), so the share says how much of the program's activity is recent. It is rough: a critical is worth 8 lows in points but often far more in money, and duplicates earn points but no bounty. The boards list at most 100 researchers and some programs hide the points, so it is used only where both are whole. The 90-day board also gives the Hunters column's second line: researchers who earned points in the last 90 days (100+ on a full board).
  • Otherwise: the saved totals scaled to 90 days, once there is a week of them; until then the cell says Pending.

A program launched less than 90 days ago has had every report in the window, so its total is the figure, counted; no estimate is more than the total it comes from.

The chart starts with the first saved total rather than with the figure: dotted at first, then from the fifth day a dashed early line, each point the rise since the first saved total scaled to 90 days (≈, rougher the fewer days it rests on). A figure with only one day saved has a dotted line beside its number until the next day's starts its chart. Intigriti's paid in 90 days and Bugcrowd's estimate from rewarded reports are drawn the same way.

Stats page: valid reports per month

Per-platform monthly series, currency rules, and which programs are included

Each platform counts valid reports its own way, so the Stats page's lines show where each is going rather than how they compare.

  • HackerOne: reports resolved on Hacktivity, by the month of their latest public activity.
  • Bugcrowd: reports accepted on CrowdStream, by the month accepted (public programs that list their reports).
  • Intigriti: the rises in each program's published count of accepted reports, saved daily. Months before the first save, up to a year back, are estimated (≈) from the program's leaderboard for the month: researchers earn a fixed number of points per accepted report by severity (and a fifth of it per duplicate), so the month's points times the program's accepted reports per point (all time) estimate its accepted reports. Where a program hides its points, the researchers who earned any that month are counted at the platform's average accepted reports per researcher a month. The leaderboards list at most 100 researchers, so the busiest months may be a little low.
  • YesWeHack: each program's Thanks list names its hunters with their valid reports; added up, that is the program's valid reports all time. HuntDeck saves the total daily and counts its rises. Months before the first save are estimated (≈): the reports the program received in the month (YesWeHack's own count for the calendar month) times its valid share, its valid reports over every report it has received. Programs that hide their Thanks list aren't counted, and the Dojo (practice challenges) is left out.

Beside them, bounties per month: on HackerOne the amounts Hacktivity shows (programs that hide amounts count each bounty at their average bounty, ≈), on Bugcrowd those CrowdStream shows, and on Intigriti what programs paid: the rises in their published total paid from the first save, and before it (≈) the month's estimated accepted reports times what the program pays per accepted report. Each is in the currency most is paid in, the rest beside it. Only programs that pay bounties now count, as for paid in 90 days: a program that stopped keeps its reports in the valid count but not its bounties.

Which programs count. The per-month numbers (valid reports, bounties, assets added to scope, programs that added scope, launches, disclosures) count every program listed now, paused and closed ones included: they describe the platform's activity, and a program paused today still had last month's reports. Open programs and paying ones are those open at each month's end; assets and wildcards in scope are today's open programs' at each month's end. Wildcards in scope are wildcard targets, once per program and value, whatever type of scope entry lists them, as the Targets page and the hunt board count them. The 90-day numbers (reports received, paid) are the Programs list's figures for open programs, added up, so the two agree; their lines are linked day to day over every listed program saved both days and scaled to end at that figure. Ignored programs count everywhere on the Stats page, which describes the platforms, not your list (the hunt board and Targets page leave them out).

A platform's own page, sorted by paid in 90 days or bounties, lists the programs paying in its lead currency (the one its line follows) first, then each other currency's, each sorted within itself, since euros and dollars can't be compared.

Lines start with the first month or day HuntDeck has. Numbers that are only a few weeks old, or estimated, stay out of the All platforms row until they cover half a year. All, Public or Private beside the title narrows every number to those programs, private being the ones you were invited to; a program counts as it is now, so one that went private counts as private in every month.

Valid · 90 days

H1 Hacktivity & BC CrowdStream counted directly; YWH & Intigriti estimated via accepted share

Reports a program accepted as valid in the last 90 days. Where a platform lists its reports, HuntDeck keeps every resolved one, with its date, and counts those resolved in the 90 days up to each day.

  • HackerOne: the undisclosed reports on Hacktivity, dated by their latest public activity (usually the resolution, sometimes a bounty awarded later). Disclosed reports are kept separately and not counted, about 3% of resolutions. A program's history is read back up to 5,000 reports when HuntDeck first sees it, which only shortens the oldest history of the very largest programs.
  • Bugcrowd: CrowdStream, dated by when each accepted report was closed. It covers the last six months of accepted reports and only public programs, so a report accepted earlier than that and resolved recently isn't counted.

YesWeHack and Intigriti list no reports, but publish how many reports each program has received and how many it accepted, all time (YesWeHack's adds up the program's Thanks list). The column shows an estimate instead, marked ≈: reports received in 90 days (as above) times the share of all the program's reports it accepted. Its line is each day's reports received times that day's accepted share, so it follows the reports-received line; the program page shows the share as Accepted share.

The charts

90-day daily sparklines scaled to range; ±5% directional color threshold

Each chart covers the last 90 days, one point a day, scaled to its own range, so it shows the direction rather than the size; hover it for the numbers. Green means it went up and red that it went down, whatever the number (more reports received is green too, though it means more competition), grey a change under 5% either way. The change compares with 90 days before, or with the first day there is when the history is shorter.

Scope changed

Latest poll-observed diff or known platform asset addition/removal date

When the program's scope last changed: the latest of the additions, removals and edits HuntDeck saw in its polls and the dates the platform's scope history gives (an asset added on a known day, or one removed). An asset already there when the history starts has only an "on or before" date, which doesn't count; a program with neither shows a dash.

In the API: estimates and freshness

v2 metadata objects (estimated, source, updated_at), since filtering, and ETag / 304

/api/v2/programs, /api/v2/invites and /api/v2/programs/{platform}/{handle} give each number that can be estimated or that polls refresh (the bounty range, the scope counts and every figure under numbers) as an object; v1 keeps them flat:

"paid_90d": {"value": 41500, "estimated": false, "source": "hackerone", "updated_at": "2026-11-03T09:00:12.345Z"}

estimated is true where the cell above shows ≈: Bugcrowd's total paid and paid in 90 days (it publishes neither; it has no count of reports received, so that value is null), YesWeHack and Intigriti received and paid in 90 days until 90 days of their totals are saved, and their valid reports in 90 days, and a bounty range read from the most a program has paid rather than its bounty table. source is the platform for its own figures, derived for what HuntDeck counts or works out (valid reports from Hacktivity or CrowdStream, figures from saved totals, every estimate) and manual for programs you added by hand. updated_at is when the value last changed, to the poll that saw it change: a figure read again unchanged keeps its time. It's null where HuntDeck can't date it: unchanged since it began keeping these times, with no saved history to date it from.

Each program also has updated_at, the last time anything about it changed (first seen, a change in Changes, one of its numbers, its removal), and last_synced_at, the last time HuntDeck read it from its platform, whether anything changed or not. since on the program lists keeps those changed after a time; on /api/v1/targets it keeps targets added or edited after it. Program, list and match answers carry an ETag: sent back as If-None-Match, it gets 304 Not Modified and no body while nothing changed.

HD=http://127.0.0.1:8080   # this HuntDeck
curl -s -D h.txt "$HD/api/v2/programs?since=2026-11-01T00:00:00Z" -o programs.json
etag=$(sed -n 's/^[Ee][Tt]ag: //p' h.txt | tr -d '\r')
curl -s -o /dev/null -w '%{http_code}\n' -H "If-None-Match: $etag" "$HD/api/v2/programs?since=2026-11-01T00:00:00Z"   # 304 until something changes
